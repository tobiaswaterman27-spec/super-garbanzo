# OUTLAW, every job in Eldoria

Money is in pence (d). 12d = 1 shilling. A labourer's day is about 6d, a loaf about 1d, an ale 1d, a tavern meal 3d, and a cottage rents for 2-5d a week.
**Wage** is a rough day's pay for a typical worker. Owners take no wage: they keep the profit after costs, so their figure is a rough profit.
**[exists]** means the job is in the game now. **[NEW]** means it is proposed for the jobs build.

Every business has a **business fund**. The owner sets the money aside, every worker on shift can spend it on what the business needs, and the owner can use it at any time and top it up.
**Needs** lists what a business must buy or fetch, and who supplies it. **Makes** lists what it turns that into.

## 3. Local government (every settlement has one)

The leader can hire, promote, demote and give raises, just like a business owner. Each week, on a set day and at a set time, every leader rides or walks to the royal castle for the **council**. They sit at the long table in the great hall with the monarch, with guards patrolling, and argue over laws, guards, buildings, money, wars, criminals and expansion. The monarch may raise or cut a leader's allowance, or sack them. A sacked or resigning leader means an **election the next day** in the government building. Citizens (people whose current home is there) vote at the ballot box. Each candidate puts themselves forward with a short record of what they've done. NPCs vote by what they want and who they like. The winner starts the next day. Until then the place runs on and wages are still paid.

## How jobs will work (rules to build)

- **Hiring.** You can ask for work even when the person isn't on duty. They can refuse depending on your reputation, whether they like you, how desperate they are, and whether they need anyone. NPC employers hire by the same rules.
- **Doing the job.** Your tasks appear in a checklist in the corner, with your next shift (when, where, and details). You may go out and buy materials with the business fund, or stay and make things as orders come in. Nothing is instant: fires die down and need firewood, and food cooks in pots over the fire.
- **Lateness.** Being late may get you fired, sometimes without being told. You can send a letter instead. Desperate or fond employers may keep you on, but missed shifts slowly make them like you less. Once fired, you're unlikely to be rehired.
- **Promotion.** Turning up and doing lots of tasks leads to promotion, depending on the business's wealth and whether the owner likes you.
- **Owners** can hire, fire, promote, demote, give raises, sell or relocate the business, and rent premises. They can buy improvements, take and sell items, and redo the interior, floors and wallpaper (the same goes for home owners and renters).
- **Tax collectors and landlords** go up to people (sprinting if they must) and demand tax or rent. Anyone who refuses ends up in jail at the guards' hands.
